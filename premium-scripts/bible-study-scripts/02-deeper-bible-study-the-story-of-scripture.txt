Deeper Bible Study 2: The Story of Scripture
Length: About 40 minutes
Format: Big-picture teaching
Primary Scripture: Luke 24:27

Opening:
Welcome to The Story of Scripture.

The Bible is not a random collection of spiritual sayings. It is one unified story of God's creation, humanity's fall, God's redemption, and the promised restoration of all things through Christ.

Pray:
Jesus, teach me to see Your presence and purpose throughout Scripture. Amen.

Read Luke 24:27.

Teaching:
After His resurrection, Jesus walked with two disciples on the road to Emmaus. Luke tells us that beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, Jesus interpreted to them the things concerning Himself in all the Scriptures.

This does not mean every verse is a secret code. It means the whole story finds its center, fulfillment, and hope in Christ.

The story begins with creation.
God creates the world good. Humanity is made in God's image, designed for relationship with Him, with one another, and with creation. Creation tells us that God is purposeful, generous, holy, and good.

Then comes the fall.
Sin enters through distrust and disobedience. Relationship with God is fractured. Shame, blame, pain, death, and brokenness spread. The fall explains why the world is beautiful and wounded at the same time.

Then redemption unfolds.
God calls Abraham. God forms Israel. God gives covenant, law, sacrifice, priesthood, kings, prophets, and promises. These are not disconnected religious pieces. They carry forward God's rescue plan.

Redemption culminates in Jesus.
Jesus is the true Son, true King, true sacrifice, true priest, true temple, true wisdom, and true image of God. Through His life, death, and resurrection, sin and death are defeated, and people are reconciled to God.

Then restoration is promised.
The Bible ends not with souls escaping creation, but with God making all things new. Revelation points to the fullness of God's kingdom, where His people dwell with Him forever.

Guided reflection:
Think of a passage you know well. Ask where it fits:
Creation: What good design is shown?
Fall: What brokenness or sin is revealed?
Redemption: What rescue, promise, sacrifice, or grace appears?
Restoration: What hope points forward?

Reflection questions:
Where do I most often forget the larger story?
How does seeing Jesus at the center change how I read the Bible?
What part of the story do I need to remember in this season?

Practice:
Choose one Bible passage this week and map it into creation, fall, redemption, and restoration.

Closing prayer:
Jesus, teach me to see Your presence and purpose throughout Scripture. Help me read every passage within Your larger story of love, holiness, rescue, and restoration. Amen.
